What is new in this edition
Two new model lines, both swept across reasoning effort through their own CLI adapters. Opus 5 (5 runs, claude-code) is the successor to the Opus 4.8 sweep already in the benchmark and can be compared to it effort-for-effort. Muse Spark 1.2 (4 runs, Meta's Model API via the new muse adapter) has no predecessor here; its effort scale tops out at xhigh, so it has no max point. All nine were audited from their on-disk artifacts against the same fact dossier.
Headlines: every Opus 5 run ships a dedicated edit-lock skill — the first family to cover elocks at every effort; Opus 5 medium is the new field leader at 9.08 with zero harness-named facets and a 1-in-295 broken-link rate; and Opus 5 high finds two /e preg_replace sites in html_entity_decode_utf8() that the dossier itself did not contain. Against that: Opus 5's max run misses the Db::Update landmine its own medium/high/xhigh all caught and leaks 11 sandbox paths, and the whole Muse line carries the harness mariadb naming into its KB taxonomy.
Read the cost columns carefully this edition
Four of the five Opus 5 runs executed across a 48-hour window in which the worker was restarted repeatedly, orphaning 20–30 in-flight CLI invocations each. Their billed compute (163–294 min) is therefore mostly re-run work, not model cost. This edition adds a second measured column — live compute, summed over non-superseded invocations only — and uses it for the charts and the value metric. For 35 of the 44 earlier runs the two figures are identical or within 5%; nine diverge more, five of them by over 20%.

What got better

  • Opus 5 raises the ceiling and the floor at once. Opus 4.8 ran 7.93–8.55 across four efforts; Opus 5 runs 8.18–9.08 across five, so its worst run beats 4.8's worst by 0.25. Effort-for-effort the gain is +0.72 at low, +0.53 at medium, +0.03 at high and +0.40 at xhigh.
  • The elocks subsystem now has family-wide coverage, not one-off coverage. All five Opus 5 runs ship a dedicated edit-lock skill, and three go beyond describing thememanager_admin.php:844 to warning against the naive “fix”: inverting it to timeout < stops it deleting the caller's own fresh row before re-insert, producing duplicate locks. That is the first time the benchmark has seen a run reason about the consequence of the bug rather than just name it.
  • The dossier itself got extended. Opus 5 high documents preg_replace with the /e modifier inside html_entity_decode_utf8() (core_functions.php:76-77) and ties it to its real call sites in actions/admin_save_form.php. Verified: those are two genuine /e sites outside the canonical six at functions.php:368-373. Only Fable medium had previously found them.
  • Citation resolution stayed high at much larger volume. Opus 5 emits 1,061–2,612 file:line citations per run against Kimi K3's 720, and four of the five resolve at 99.9–100%.

What got worse / didn't move

  • Top effort still regresses, now with a sharper edge. Opus 5 max is the family's worst run (8.18): it misses the Db::Update landmine that medium, high and xhigh all caught, and it ships 11 literal /haive/workdir paths inside the page-alias-routing skill — more than double Sonnet-5 max's five. The pattern (max buys volume and defects, not correctness) now holds for five of six claude-family sweeps.
  • Global routing is still the ceiling, and Opus 5 shows it is not an effort problem. 41 of 53 runs promoted nothing. Opus 5 promotes 3 clean entries at low and 3 at medium, then zero at high, xhigh and max — the three runs that produced the largest, most accurate local corpora. Whatever gates promotion, more reasoning is not it.
  • A new failure mode: plausible line numbers for the wrong function. Muse Spark 1.2's structural citation health looks fine (0–3 out-of-range lines per run) but hand-verification of muse-high found four citations naming a real file and an in-range line that belongs to a different function entirely. Every automated check in this benchmark would pass that.
  • Harness bleed found a new surface again. Muse puts MariaDB 10.11 in the DEPLOYMENT.md title in two of four runs — not a facet name, not a prose slip, but the document's own statement of what the project targets.

09Notable defects & patterns

The Db::Update landmine

Still the single most discriminating fact. Update() returns true on failure (database.php:456) and false on success (:459). Codex 5.6 catches it at all six efforts — the only family to do so. Opus 5 catches it at medium, high and xhigh but not at low or max; its xhigh write-up is the field's best, a dedicated ANTI_PATTERNS section headed “Db::Update() returns false on SUCCESS” that cites :456 vs :459, contrasts Insert/Delete/Truncate, and adds the operational advice that existing callers ignore the return value and new code should too. Muse catches it at low and medium only. Haiku low remains the only run to actively reverse it.
Jest trap — three July runs materialised a jest-specialist for a project with no tests. None of the nine new runs falls for it; all nine write a TESTING_STANDARDS.md asserting no test infrastructure exists. Opus 5 low and medium go furthest, naming yui/yuitest/ as the vendored false signal, and Opus 5 max additionally verifies the absence of .ddev/ in the pristine tree. None names the Polish/Slovenian “jest” string in the bundled FCKeditor language files, so Codex-5.6 xhigh keeps the definitive debunk.
Line numbers that pass a checker and fail a reader. The new failure mode of this round. muse-high cites ReplaceLinksToOtherPages at functions.php:235-242 and :220-250 (the function is at :364; :235-242 is ConvertMonth()), the GetPHPVariables eval at core_functions.php:95 (real: :140), and the [*dtt:*] eval at functions.php:145 (real: :328). All four name a real file and an in-range line. The structural citation check — the one that produced this report's 100%-resolvable figures — cannot see them.
Phantom filenames still appear at the top of the field. Opus 5 high cites admins_manage.php four times; the real file is mods/admin_class_admins_manage.php and no admins_manage.php exists anywhere in the tree. These are the only 4 unresolvable citations in its 2,193 — but they sit in the rank/ACL documentation, the exact place a reader would follow them.

Hygiene & routing

Sandbox-path leaks: Opus 5 max sets a new field record at 11 literal /haive/workdir paths, all inside page-alias-routing and most of them as cd /haive/workdir in runnable command blocks. Prior worsts: Kimi K2.7 (Jul-10) 9, Sonnet-5 max 5, Fable xhigh 4. The other four Opus 5 runs and all four Muse runs are leak-free.
Harness-value bleed keeps finding new surfaces. All four Muse runs name mariadb KB facets, and two put MariaDB 10.11 in the DEPLOYMENT.md title. Opus 5 xhigh files a mariadb facet alongside its ext-mysql one — accurate schema content, harness-derived name, so it caps detection rather than KB. Opus 5 medium is the cleanest run in 53: no harness-named facet anywhere, and version-pinned names (php-mysql-ext, fckeditor2, jquery-1-2, yui2) that match the bundled artefacts exactly (verified: jQuery 1.2.1/1.2.3, YUI 2.3.1).
Broken cross-skill links remain endemic and uncorrelated with spend (0 to 44 per run). The deterministic verify step checks structure, not link targets. Opus 5 low, high and xhigh post perfect 0/156, 0/323 and 0/307 — the largest clean link sets in the benchmark. Opus 5 max breaks 6 of 330 by inventing six skill directory names that were never created (../edit-locks/, ../rank-visibility/, ../alias-routing/…), the same failure shape as DeepSeek's field-worst 44/272.
Effort past the peak buys defects, not quality — now with a sixth family confirming it. Opus 5 peaks at medium (9.08) and falls to 8.48 / 8.45 / 8.18 at high / xhigh / max while live compute rises from 37.8 to 104.8 minutes at xhigh. Every xhigh run in the benchmark lands below its family peak. The single counterexample remains Codex-5.6 max.

10Recommendations

  1. New default for highest-stakes onboarding: Opus 5 at medium effort. Top composite of all 53 runs (9.08) for 37.8 live CLI minutes — less compute than the previous leader. It is the only run with zero harness-named facets, version-pinned KB taxonomy, 1 broken link in 295, 2,209 citations with none unresolvable, the Db::Update landmine documented with the Insert contrast, a dedicated edit-lock skill, and three correctly-faceted global promotions. Nothing in this benchmark currently beats it on any dimension except raw promotion count.
  2. Best value is unchanged: Sonnet 5 at medium — 8.38 in 26.4 live minutes (0.317 composite/min), still the strongest A-tier quality per compute minute in the benchmark. Opus 5 medium buys +0.70 composite for +11.4 minutes (0.240/min), which is the right trade for a repo you will live in and the wrong one for a throwaway. Below the A tier, Muse Spark 1.2 at low is the cheapest usable run in the benchmark (7.68 in 15.6 minutes, 0.492/min) and it does catch the Update landmine.
  3. When accuracy on API contracts matters most: Codex gpt-5.6-sol or Opus 5 xhigh. Codex 5.6 is still the only family catching the Db::Update inverted return at every effort. Opus 5 xhigh writes the single best version of that finding and adds the sharpest CSRF framing in the field (“there is no per-request CSRF token”), but costs 104.8 live minutes for a lower composite than its own medium.
  4. Muse Spark 1.2 is a budget option, not a quality option. It averages about a third of the Opus 5 sweep's compute per run (18.8 live minutes against 56.6) and never fabricates an API, but it caps at 3–4 skills, names the harness database in its taxonomy, and — the disqualifier for unattended use — produces line citations that point at the wrong function while passing every automated check. If you use it, pin low or medium (its two best runs, and the only two that catch the Update landmine) and budget human citation review.
  5. The effort dial: stop at each family's peak — never xhigh. No family peaks at xhigh, and only Codex 5.6 rewards max. Opus 4.8, Opus 5 and Fable all peak at medium. For Opus 5 specifically, going medium→max costs 22 extra live minutes and 0.90 composite, and turns 0 sandbox leaks into 11.
  6. Do not use Haiku 4.5 for legacy onboarding (best 6.75; reverses the Update contract, fabricates escaping, hallucinates a Jest agent at max), and treat Muse Spark 1.2 at high (6.95) as the same tier for the citation reasons above.

▸ Methodology & caveats
  • Ground truth and source identity. Every one of the 53 leaderboard runs onboarded a byte-identical copy of the same Redaction System source. This was re-verified for this edition, not assumed: for each repository the import commit's tree was listed with git ls-tree -r, Haive-generated paths (.claude/, .agents/, .codex/, .haive*, .ddev/, AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md, .gitignore) were excluded, and the remaining 828 application blobs were hashed. All produce the identical digest. One new repository needed a different method: rs_claude_opus5_low has no .git directory at all (it predates the worker fix that runs git init when onboarding commits a repo with no history), so there is no import commit to list. It was verified blob-by-blob instead: each of the 828 reference paths was hashed on disk with git hash-object and compared to the reference tree — 828 matched, 0 differed, 0 missing. Because that repo also had no later task run against it, its working tree is its post-onboarding state. Two further candidates, rs_test_gl and rs_test_gh (14 July, Haiku Low), are excluded on provenance: LF-normalised copies of the same corpus, created back-to-back as duplicate Haiku-Low controls for the global-KB supersede path, not distinct model or effort points.
  • Auditing the right bytes. The import commit in these repositories is written after onboarding finishes, so it already contains .claude/ and .agents/; later tasks against the same repo add further commits. Every repository was therefore checked for post-onboarding drift before auditing. The four Muse repositories each carry one later “add README” workflow commit that touches README.md and nothing else, and the four git-backed Opus 5 repositories have exactly one commit each — so the on-disk .claude/ tree is the onboarding output in all nine cases. (For contrast, the reference repo rs_claude_sonnet_high has a later commit that rewrites functions.php's /e preg_replace calls into preg_replace_callback; source-fact checks were run against a pristine tree, not that one.)
  • Audit dossier. Findings were judged against the real files and one shared, source-verified fact dossier, every item re-confirmed against a pristine tree for this edition: the Db::Update inverted return (return true on error at database.php:456, return false on success at :459), the elocks table and its asymmetric freshness handling (the own-row Delete at thememanager_admin.php:844 filters timeout > $time_delete; the contention SELECT at :845 has no freshness filter at all), SECHASH = md5($_SERVER['HTTP_USER_AGENT'].$_SERVER['REMOTE_ADDR']) at installer/init.php:143 (IP/UA binding, not a CSRF token), no rank/CSRF API, encryptPW($pw, $salt = 'salted_sardine') = md5(crypt($pw, substr($salt,0,2))) at functions.php:988-990, the is_numeric-guarded math-captcha eval('$r = '.$s1.$sg.$s2.';') at functions.php:1301, the six /e preg_replace sites at functions.php:368-373, the fckeditor escaping bypass (strpos(strtolower($value),'fckeditor')===false guards at core_functions.php:143 and :151), RS_CODENAME=atlantis/RS_VERSION=beta2 at installer/init.php:139-140, and the 23-table schema (23 CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS statements in installer/rs2.php, counted). Two dossier items were extended by this round's evidence and then verified independently: there are further /e sites outside the canonical six — core_functions.php:76 and :77 inside html_entity_decode_utf8(), and actions/admin_remove_cpage.php:81 — and History::Add() carries an inverted guard at history.php:52-54 (if (in_array($code, $this->codes)) { $code = 0; } rewrites a valid code to the error code, the opposite of its own docblock). Artifact count alone earned no credit.
  • Link and citation measurement. Measured, not estimated, by one script run over all nine new runs and both August-03 runs. Every relative markdown link under .claude/knowledge_base/ and .claude/skills/ is resolved against the filesystem; every file.php:NNN citation is checked for file existence (matching by basename anywhere in the repo, so a bare form_submit.php resolves to actions/form_submit.php) and for the line number falling within the file. The script was calibrated before use by reproducing the published August-03 figures: DeepSeek V4 Flash 44/272 links and 511 citations exactly, Kimi K3 5/109 links exactly (its citation count re-measures at 720 against the published 715 — a 0.7% regex difference, noted rather than retro-fitted).
  • What the citation figure does and does not prove. It is a structural check: the file exists and the line is inside it. It cannot detect a citation that names a real file and an in-range line belonging to a different function, which is exactly the defect found by hand in muse-high. An anchored-precision metric was built for this edition (match a symbol on the same line to its ground-truth span) and discarded: lines naming two symbols cross-attribute their citations, and one ground-truth span used to build it was itself wrong, so it produced false misses in runs that were correct. Where this report calls a citation wrong, a human opened the cited file. Treat the per-run citation percentages as a floor on accuracy, never a ceiling.
  • Model identity. Verified from each run's CLI init event rather than the task title or the cli_providers.model text column, which is known to go stale (the z.ai rows still read glm-4.6 while the runs were glm-5.2[1m]). The five Opus 5 runs echo "model":"claude-opus-5" and the four Muse runs "model":"muse-spark-1.2" in the system/init event of their stream logs. That check matters this round: the Muse Spark 1.2 xHigh provider row has an empty model column and picks the model up from the adapter default, so the DB text field alone would have identified it as nothing at all.
  • Effort scales are not the same length. claude-code exposes low/medium/high/xhigh/max, so Opus 5 is a five-point sweep. The muse adapter exposes low/medium/high/xhigh only — Meta's Model API rejects max with the same 400 an unknown value gets — so Muse Spark 1.2 is a four-point sweep with no max row, not a missing run. Codex 5.6 additionally has ultra. Cross-family comparisons in section 06 are therefore made at high, the one level every swept family has.
  • Detection (20%). Audited LLM output plus AGENTS.md/CLAUDE.md; deterministic confirmation-form values (mariadb 10.11, apache) were separated from model judgment, and copying them into LLM-authored KB was penalised.
  • KB (25%), Skills (25%), Agents (15%). Read every KB file (recursive), every SKILL.md, and only the accepted.source = "llm" discovered agents (the ~28 shared scan/template agents excluded), read from the 06_5-agent-discovery step output rather than inferred from filenames. The nine new runs discovered 3–8 each; every one was checked for grounding against the real tree before counting (e.g. Opus 5 xhigh's yui2-specialist and legacy-php4-specialist are both real — YUI 2.3.1 is bundled and fckeditor.php dispatches to fckeditor_php4.php at runtime).
  • Global KB (15%). Counted task-attributed promotions in haive_kb_global.global_kb_entries across every status. The query was calibrated against published values before use (Sonnet-5 high = 4, Codex-5.6 high = 6, Opus 4.8 xhigh = 1, all exact). Zero promotions = the 6.0 neutral floor (no contamination, missed reuse), not a six-out-of-ten quality verdict. Opus 5 low and medium returned 3 rows each; the other seven new runs returned zero. Promotion content was read, not just counted: Opus 5 medium's three ext/mysql articles are portable, project-free and correctly faceted (php 5 + mysql/mariadb), while Opus 5 low's jQuery 1.2 cheat sheet is accurate but faceted language: php / framework: general — a routing error that caps its Global score below medium's.
  • Cost — two columns this edition. Billed = sum of cli_invocations.duration_ms over all rows including superseded retries, the rule used by every prior edition. Live = the same sum restricted to superseded_at IS NULL. Both queries were validated by reproducing published figures exactly before use (Sonnet-5 high 24 inv / 40.5 min / 184,147 tok; retry counts 5/2/7/1 for Sonnet-5 max, Sonnet-4.6 high, Sonnet-4.6 max and Codex-5.5 low; all six July-10 runs to the decimal). Why the second column exists: Opus 5 medium/high/xhigh/max were retried 56/45/39/55 times, and the retry reasons were read rather than assumed — 20–30 per run are CLI invocation orphaned by a worker restart, 15–22 more are superseded rows that had already exited 0, and only 4–7 per run are provider rate-limit exhaustion. That is harness instability across a 48-hour window, not model cost, and billing it as model compute would have put the round's best run at 163 minutes instead of 38. Charts and the value metric use Live; the leaderboard shows both. Raw input/total tokens remain excluded (providers cache differently).
  • Wall time is contextual only and omitted from rankings. The four long Opus 5 runs show 2,763–3,016 wall minutes (~46–50 h) against 38–105 live compute minutes — the restart window plus unattended form-gate idle. Kimi K3's 1,507 wall minutes and the three gpt-5.5 Codex runs' ~40 h are the same pattern. Speed and value use compute minutes only.
  • Pipeline drift between rounds. The step list is not frozen: the nine new runs execute 26 steps, as the August-03 runs did, against the July-13/14 runs' 25. Opus 5 low completed 22 of 26; the other eight completed 21. Cross-round deltas therefore mix model change with pipeline change and are reported per model line rather than pooled. The Opus 4.8 → Opus 5 comparison in section 02 crosses that boundary and should be read as generation-plus-pipeline, not generation alone.
  • Calibration. Dimension scores are evidence-calibrated 0.5-point judgments, not measurements; composite decimals come only from the published weights. The nine new runs were scored against the previously published runs as fixed anchors, including the rule that a harness-named facet whose content is accurate caps detection rather than KB (as applied to Sonnet-5 high, Codex-5.6 low and Kimi K3). Two scores exceed the previous field maximum of 9.0 — Opus 5 medium's detection and KB, both at 9.5 — and both are argued rather than asserted: its detection beats the previous best on every axis that best run was docked for (zero harness-named facets vs two; version-pinned facet names verified against the bundled artefacts), and its KB matches the previous best on landmine coverage while beating it on breadth, link integrity (1/295 vs ~20) and citation resolution.
