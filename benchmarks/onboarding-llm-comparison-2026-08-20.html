Where Opus 4.6 loses the points

  • The knowledge base evaporates as effort rises. 30 KB files at low, then 16 / 18 / 17 / 12. The max run has one file:line citation in the entire knowledge_base/ and no facet subdirectories at all — no TECH_PATTERNS, no ANTI_PATTERNS, no BEST_PRACTICES, no QUICK_REFERENCE. Its skills are fine; its KB is a set of loose prose documents.
  • A fabricated table prefix, stated as fact. opus46-high writes “The schema uses rs2_ prefix by default (visible in installer/rs2.php)” and then names every table rs2_* — 160+ occurrences. Verified: installer/rs2.php contains only ###PREFIX### placeholders, and the installer's default value is rs_ (installer/contents_step_4.php:40), chosen by the operator at install time. Every one of those table names is unresolvable against a real database, and no structural checker sees it.
  • The Db::Update landmine is caught at the ends and missed in the middle — low gets it in the DB cheat-sheet, max in API_REFERENCE, and medium/high/xhigh all document Update() without a word about the inverted return.
  • Two discovered agents at four of five efforts. Only low reaches four (adding fckeditor and jquery-legacy); everything above it stops at legacy-php + mysql.
  • Credit where due: opus46-medium is the only Opus 4.6 run with no harness-named facet and the only run in the round to reconcile both version identities in one line (“codename calypso / atlantis beta2”); opus46-xhigh independently finds the /e sites at core_functions.php:76-77; and opus46-max's edit-lock sub-skill reports four verified nuances no other run has (admin_edit_cpage.php:162 deletes without the multipart filter, remove_locks clears every lock the user holds, getmicrotime() floats truncate into int(11), and MyISAM leaves the check-then-insert race open).

What got better

  • A second family now catches the Db::Update landmine at every effort. Since 14 July only Codex 5.6 had managed it. All four GLM-5.3 runs do: a dedicated ANTI_PATTERNS heading at medium, an inline CAUTION in API_REFERENCE plus a numbered cheat-sheet item at high, a signature note at max, and a “Silent failure modes” paragraph at low. Three of the four cite the exact range database.php:448-460.
  • The extended /e finding is now reproducible, not anecdotal. Four of the eleven new runs — glm53-low, glm53-high, glm53-max and opus46-xhigh — independently cite core_functions.php:76 inside html_entity_decode_utf8(), and opus46-xhigh lists it beside the canonical functions.php:368-373 block in two separate documents. Before this round only Fable medium and Opus 5 high had reached it.
  • The deepest single finding the benchmark has recorded. glm53-max reports that History::Add() has no call site anywhere in the codebase and that its guard can never fire, because $codes holds arrays (0 => array('History Code Not Found', 0)) so a scalar $code never matches in_array. Both halves verified against source. That goes a step past the last edition's muse-xhigh finding, which correctly called the guard inverted and stopped there.
  • Perfect citation resolution is now common at mid volume. Seven of the eleven new runs resolve 100% of their file:line citations (388–1,344 each) and four post zero broken relative links — glm53-low (0/162), opus46-low (0/127), opus46-high (0/115) and opus46-xhigh (0/86).

What got worse / didn't move

  • Top effort regresses again, and now it can take the KB with it. Opus 4.6 runs 7.83 → 7.53 → 6.88 → 7.18 → 6.98 across low/medium/high/xhigh/max. Only one other family peaks at its cheapest effort (Muse Spark 1.2, over a 0.73 spread against this 0.95). Its KB drops from 30 files to 12 over that range, its KB-level citations from 18 to 1, and its facet taxonomy from five directories to none. The pattern (past a family's peak, effort buys volume elsewhere and defects, not correctness) now holds for six of seven claude-family sweeps.
  • Global routing is still the ceiling, and it is not a capability problem. 52 of 64 runs promoted nothing, including all eleven new ones. GLM-5.3 high built the round's largest, most accurate, most densely cited corpus and promoted zero entries; so did every Opus 4.6 and Grok run. Whatever gates promotion, neither reasoning effort nor corpus quality is it.
  • A new fabrication surface: the table prefix. Runs have invented functions and tables before; opus46-high invents the naming convention, asserting rs2_ as the schema's default prefix and then applying it to every table name in 160+ places. The prefix is user-chosen at install time and the installer's default is rs_. Nothing in the structural checks can see this — the citations it hangs off resolve fine.
  • Harness bleed is smaller but not gone. Seven of eleven still file a mariadb10/mariadb-10 KB facet, which then generates its own titled documents (BEST_PRACTICES/mariadb10-best-practices.md and friends). What no run does this round is state MariaDB as the project's deployment target, the way two Muse runs titled their DEPLOYMENT.md last edition. Only three are clean: grok-build-0.1 (no mariadb or ddev string anywhere in KB or skills), opus46-medium and opus46-max.
  • Reversal errors have not disappeared. grok-build-0.1 documents History::Add() backwards (“coerce unknown to 0”; the real guard rewrites a known code), and opus46-max claims a stale lock “is removed” when another admin arrives — contradicting its own Known Limitations section three bullets later, and verified false: no code path deletes an elocks row by age.

09Notable defects & patterns

The Db::Update landmine

Still the single most discriminating fact. Update() returns true on failure (database.php:456) and false on success (:459). Codex 5.6 catches it at all six efforts and GLM-5.3 at all four — the only two families to cover their whole sweep. Opus 5 catches it at medium, high and xhigh; Muse at low and medium; Opus 4.6 only at low and max, with medium, high and xhigh all documenting Update()'s signature and saying nothing about the return. Both Grok runs catch it, and grok-4.6 writes the field's most usable version: an ANTI_PATTERNS line citing database.php:448-460 with the Insert/Delete contrast, plus a cheat-sheet callout that just says “false means success”. Haiku low remains the only run to actively reverse it.
Jest trap — three July runs materialised a jest-specialist for a project with no tests. None of the eleven new runs falls for it; all eleven write a TESTING_STANDARDS.md asserting no test infrastructure exists, and no discovered-agent list contains one. grok-4.6 and opus46-xhigh name the vendored yui/yuitest/ as the false signal; glm53-medium states it verified by find that the only test matches are AI-generated. None names the Polish/Slovenian “jest” string in the bundled FCKeditor language files, so Codex-5.6 xhigh keeps the definitive debunk.
Line numbers that pass a checker and fail a reader. Last edition's new failure mode, found by hand in muse-high (four citations naming a real file and an in-range line belonging to a different function). Hand-sampling the same way across this round found none. Every citation opened from glm53-high, glm53-medium, grok-4.6, opus46-high and opus46-xhigh landed on the function it named — makeDBSafe at core_functions.php:42, RemoveHtml at :95, GetPHPVariables at :115 and :140, checkSecure at installer/init.php:210, checkInt at functions.php:82, editAccess at :685, checkAliasesCompatibility at :1315. That is a sample, not a proof: the metric remains a floor.
Phantom filenames are now the dominant unresolvable. Both Grok runs cite bare maintenance.php and log_review.php; the real files are mods/admin_class_maintenance.php and mods/admin_class_log_review.php, and no file by the bare name exists. That is 3 of grok-build's 385 citations and 2 of grok-4.6's 1,111 — small counts, but they sit in the maintenance runbook, the place a reader would follow them.

Hygiene & routing

Sandbox-path leaks: all eleven new runs are leak-free — zero literal /haive/workdir paths across every KB and skill file in the round. The field record stands at Opus 5 max's 11; prior worsts were Kimi K2.7 (Jul-10) 9, Sonnet-5 max 5, Fable xhigh 4.
Harness-value bleed narrowed but did not close. Seven of eleven file a mariadb10/mariadb-10 KB facet — accurate schema content under a harness-derived name, so it caps detection rather than KB. Only grok-build-0.1 is fully clean (no mariadb and no ddev string anywhere in KB or skills), with opus46-medium and opus46-max close behind on generic “MySQL/MariaDB” prose and no facet. grok-4.6's three DDEV mentions are the good kind: they describe the dev router forcing an https bounce, not the project's stack. Opus 5 medium remains the cleanest run in 64.
Broken cross-skill links remain endemic and uncorrelated with spend (0 to 44 per run). The deterministic verify step checks structure, not link targets. Four new runs post perfect sets — glm53-low 0/162, opus46-low 0/127, opus46-high 0/115, opus46-xhigh 0/86. At the other end glm53-high breaks 11 of 187, every one an invented sibling directory (../content-rendering-pipeline/, ../rank-acl/, ../aliases-edit-locks/), and glm53-medium breaks 7 of 179 — five of them ./sub-skills/*.md files its own SKILL.md promises and never writes, a self-referential variant of the same failure.
Effort past the peak buys defects, not quality — and Opus 4.6 shows the peak can be low. It falls 7.83 → 7.53 → 6.88 → 7.18 → 6.98 across low/medium/high/xhigh/max — not a smooth decline, but no level above low ever recovers, and the trough is high, the setting most operators reach for by default. GLM-5.3 is the counter-shape: essentially flat (8.15 / 8.05 / 8.03 / 7.83), peaking at high. Every xhigh run in the benchmark still lands below its family peak; the single counterexample at max remains Codex-5.6.

10Recommendations

  1. Unchanged for highest-stakes onboarding: Opus 5 at medium effort. Top composite of all 64 runs (9.08) for 37.8 live CLI minutes. Nothing this round came close — the best new run, GLM-5.3 high at 8.15, needs 2.3× the compute for 0.93 less composite. Opus 5 medium remains the only run with zero harness-named facets, version-pinned KB taxonomy, 1 broken link in 295, 2,209 citations with none unresolvable, a dedicated edit-lock skill, and three correctly-faceted global promotions.
  2. Best value is unchanged: Sonnet 5 at medium — 8.38 in 26.4 live minutes (0.317 composite/min). The new round's efficiency candidate is GLM-5.3 at low: 7.83 in 37.7 live minutes (0.208/min) with perfect link hygiene, 100% citation resolution and the Update landmine caught. Watch the billing though — that run's billed compute is 127.4 minutes, and if your provider bills superseded retries the value collapses to 0.061/min.
  3. The new pick when API-contract accuracy is the whole job: GLM-5.3 at high. It catches the Db::Update inverted return (as does every other GLM-5.3 effort), writes the round's deepest edit-lock analysis — explicitly refusing the “expired-lock cleanup” misreading of thememanager_admin.php:844 — and carries 227 file:line citations inside the knowledge base alone, all of them resolving. Its cost is the mariadb10 facet family and 11 broken sibling links. Codex gpt-5.6-sol is still the alternative if you want the landmine caught at every effort with cleaner links.
  4. Do not pick Opus 4.6, and if you must, pin low. The family runs 6.88–7.83, below every other claude-family sweep, and its behaviour above low is the argument: high is the family's worst run because it fabricates the rs2_ table prefix across 160+ names, max ships 12 KB files with one citation and no facet taxonomy, and medium/high/xhigh all miss the Update landmine that low catches. Opus 4.8 at matched effort is +1.02 at medium, +1.57 at high and +0.87 at xhigh for comparable compute — only at low are the two generations close (+0.10).
  5. Grok is a two-model question, not an effort question. The adapter exposes no effort dial. grok-4.6 (7.98) is a genuinely usable mid-field run — the round's cleanest detection, version-pinned facets, no mariadb anywhere, every dossier citation exact. grok-build-0.1 (6.90) is not: zero file:line citations in its entire knowledge base, one discovered agent, and History::Add() documented backwards. Its one real virtue is cost — 74.6K output tokens and zero retries, the cheapest complete run of the round.
  6. The effort dial: stop at each family's peak, and know where that peak is. It is not always medium. Opus 4.8, Opus 5 and Fable peak at medium; Sonnet 5, Sonnet 4.6, Codex 5.5 and now GLM-5.3 peak at high; Codex 5.6 at max; and Opus 4.6 and Muse Spark 1.2 peak at low. No family peaks at xhigh, and only Codex 5.6 rewards max.
  7. Do not use Haiku 4.5 for legacy onboarding (best 6.75; reverses the Update contract, fabricates escaping, hallucinates a Jest agent at max), and treat Opus 4.6 at high (6.88), grok-build-0.1 (6.90), Muse Spark 1.2 at high (6.95) and Opus 4.6 at max (6.98) as the same tier — the five lowest runs in the benchmark outside the Haiku sweep.

▸ Methodology & caveats
  • Ground truth and source identity. Every one of the 64 leaderboard runs onboarded a byte-identical copy of the same Redaction System source. This was re-verified for the eleven new repositories, not assumed: for each one the root commit's tree was listed with git ls-tree -r, Haive-generated paths were excluded, and the remaining application blobs were hashed into one digest over the sorted <blob-sha> <path> listing. All eleven produce 828 blobs and the identical digest 778d2816440fe499, as do three previously-published reference repositories re-run through the same script (rs_claude_opus5_medium, rs_muse_spark_1.2_low, rs_claude_sonnet_high). The exclusion list needed one addition this round: the pipeline now writes a .grok/ target directory (51–65 files per repo), which is Haive-generated and was pushing the raw counts to 879–898. The full exclusion set is now .claude/, .agents/, .codex/, .grok/, .gemini/, .haive*, .ddev/, AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md, GEMINI.md, .gitignore, .mcp.json. (The digest value differs from the one quoted in the 8 August edition because the hashing recipe was re-derived here and stated explicitly; what carries across editions is the 828-blob count and the fact that published reference repos hash identically to the new ones under the same script.) Two candidates remain excluded on provenance: rs_test_gl and rs_test_gh (14 July, Haiku Low), LF-normalised copies of the same corpus created back-to-back as duplicate Haiku-Low controls for the global-KB supersede path.
  • Auditing the right bytes — this matters more this round than any before it. The root commit in these repositories is written after onboarding finishes, so it already contains .claude/ and .agents/; later tasks against the same repo add further commits. Seven of the eleven new repositories carry post-onboarding workflow commits, and six of those modified .claude/knowledge_base/ — 1 to 9 files each, from later “Add README” and “Add DDEV” tasks whose docs: update knowledge base from workflow commits are exactly what the pipeline is designed to do. Auditing the working tree would therefore have scored those runs on bytes they did not write. Every figure in this edition for the eleven new runs — KB file counts, skill counts, link and citation measurements, and every quoted line of text — was taken from the tree at each repository's root commit, extracted with git archive, not from the working directory. Source-fact checks were run against a pristine tree extracted the same way from rs_glm_53_low, which has exactly one commit; that matters because rs_grok_build's later workflow commit rewrote functions.php, core_functions.php and init.php.
  • Audit dossier. Findings were judged against the real files and one shared, source-verified fact dossier, every item re-confirmed against a pristine tree for this edition: the Db::Update inverted return (return true on error at database.php:456, return false on success at :459), the elocks table and its asymmetric freshness handling (the own-row Delete at thememanager_admin.php:844 filters timeout > $time_delete; the contention SELECT at :845 has no freshness filter at all), SECHASH = md5($_SERVER['HTTP_USER_AGENT'].$_SERVER['REMOTE_ADDR']) at installer/init.php:143 (IP/UA binding, not a CSRF token), no rank/CSRF API, encryptPW($pw, $salt = 'salted_sardine') = md5(crypt($pw, substr($salt,0,2))) at functions.php:988-990, the is_numeric-guarded math-captcha eval('$r = '.$s1.$sg.$s2.';') at functions.php:1301, the six /e preg_replace sites at functions.php:368-373, the fckeditor escaping bypass (strpos(strtolower($value),'fckeditor')===false guards at core_functions.php:143 and :151), RS_CODENAME=atlantis/RS_VERSION=beta2 at installer/init.php:139-140, and the 23-table schema (23 CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS statements in installer/rs2.php, counted). Two dossier items were extended by the 8 August round and then verified independently: there are further /e sites outside the canonical six — core_functions.php:76 and :77 inside html_entity_decode_utf8(), and actions/admin_remove_cpage.php:81 — and History::Add() carries an inverted guard at history.php:52-54. Three more items were added this round, each verified against the pristine tree before any run was credited or docked for it. (1) History::Add() has no call site anywhere: no new History, no $history, no ->Add( outside history.php itself, and the guard can never fire because $codes holds arrays (0 => array('History Code Not Found', 0)), so a scalar $code never matches in_array. (2) No code path deletes an elocks row by age.   • Link and citation measurement. Measured, not estimated, by one script run over the eleven new runs. Every relative markdown link under .claude/knowledge_base/ and .claude/skills/ is resolved against the filesystem; every file.php:NNN citation is checked for file existence (matching by basename anywhere in the repo, so a bare form_submit.php resolves to actions/form_submit.php) and for the line number falling within the file. The script was calibrated before use by re-measuring all nine August-06/07 runs and reproducing their published figures: seven of nine match exactly on all four numbers (opus5-low 0/156 & 1,061 citations; opus5-medium 1/295 & 2,209; opus5-xhigh 0/307 & 2,516; all four Muse runs), and the remaining two differ by at most 2 citations out of 2,193 and 2,612 (opus5-high, opus5-max) — a <0.1% regex difference, noted rather than retro-fitted. Sandbox-path leak counts were re-measured by the same script and reproduce exactly (Opus 5 max 11, everything else 0).
  • What the citation figure does and does not prove. It is a structural check: the file exists and the line is inside it. It cannot detect a citation that names a real file and an in-range line belonging to a different function, which is exactly the defect found by hand in muse-high last edition. Nor can it detect a fabricated identifier that never appears in a citation at all — which is how opus46-high's invented rs2_ table prefix passes a 0/115 link score and a 99.86% citation resolution. An anchored-precision metric was built for the previous edition and discarded as unreliable (lines naming two symbols cross-attribute their citations). Where this report calls something wrong, a human opened the cited file. Treat the per-run citation percentages as a floor on accuracy, never a ceiling.
  • Model identity. Verified per invocation rather than from the task title or the cli_providers.model text column, which is known to go stale. Two channels were read, and this round they agree everywhere. For GLM-5.3 the recorded cli_invocations.model_identity shows requested: glm-5.3[1m] against served: glm-5.3 on 98 invocations across the four runs — match: exact, because the endpoint drops a trailing variant tag while naming the same model. All exact. Why the gap looks different this round: the 157 superseded rows across the eleven new runs break down as 48 that had already exited 0 and were re-run anyway (288.8 min, 737,185 output tokens), 20 provider rate-limit (93.8 min, 24,809 tokens), 27 orphaned by a worker restart — of which 24 never started and cost 0 ms — 60 carrying no message at all, of which 53 never started, and 2 cancel/timeout (12.0 min). Cross-round deltas therefore mix model change with pipeline change and are reported per model line rather than pooled. The eleven new runs were scored against the previously published runs as fixed anchors, including the rule that a harness-named facet whose content is accurate caps detection rather than KB (as applied to Sonnet-5 high, Codex-5.6 low, Kimi K3 and Opus 5 xhigh, and here to seven of the eleven).
